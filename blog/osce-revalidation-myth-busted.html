Does NMC Revalidation Mean You Have to Retake the OSCE?

No. NMC revalidation never involves the OSCE — not now, not ever.

If you've been losing sleep over this, you're not alone. But the confusion is costing you time you could spend on what actually matters. Here's the truth, and how Revalidation Copilot handles the rest.

Why Every Nurse Has Heard This Rumor

The OSCE (Objective Structured Clinical Examination) and NMC revalidation both involve the Nursing and Midwifery Council. Both happen on a cycle. Both feel like tests. So it's easy to see why the two get mixed up — especially when you're scrolling through forums at 11pm wondering if you're about to be blindsided.

The reality is simpler than the rumor makes it feel. They are two entirely different NMC processes, and revalidation has never required a clinical skills assessment of any kind.

What NMC Revalidation Actually Requires

Over your three-year cycle, you need to gather evidence across eight areas. None of them is an exam.

  • 450 revalidation practice hours (900 if you hold dual registration) — tracked and logged
  • 35 CPD hours, of which at least 20 must be participatory learning — planned and recorded
  • 5 pieces of practice-related feedback — collected from colleagues, patients, or supervisors
  • 5 written reflective accounts — each linking your CPD or feedback to the NMC Code
  • 1 reflective discussion — with another NMC-registered professional
  • Confirmation from a confirmer — usually your line manager, signing off that you meet the standards
  • A health and character declaration
  • Evidence of professional indemnity cover

Revalidation is about evidence, not exams. It's built from your day-to-day practice, not a test hall.

When the OSCE Actually Comes Up

The OSCE is part of NMC registration, not revalidation. It applies in two narrow situations:

SituationOSCE required?Why
You're an internationally educated nurse applying for the first time Yes The OSCE is part of the Test of Competence, which is required to join the NMC register
Your registration has lapsed for 5+ years Yes — you'll need a return to practice course or the full Test of Competence After a long gap, the NMC needs to confirm your clinical competence before readmission
You're a UK-trained nurse doing routine revalidation No Revalidation is paperwork, not an exam. Period.
Your registration lapsed recently (within a few months) No You complete outstanding revalidation requirements plus a readmission application
If you're an internationally educated nurse: The OSCE is part of your initial registration, not your three-yearly revalidation cycle. Once you're on the register, revalidation is about evidence of ongoing practice — not retaking exams. Read the full IEN revalidation guide →

The Real Source of the Confusion

The confusion usually comes from two places:

  1. Both processes involve the NMC — so when one nurse mentions "the NMC process," another hears "revalidation" when they mean "registration."
  2. The word "competence" appears in both — the OSCE tests clinical competence; revalidation confirms you're fit to practise. They sound similar. They are not the same thing.
